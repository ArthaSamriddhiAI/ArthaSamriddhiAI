# Character Bible: Mrs. Lalitha Iyengar (Archetype 01)

**Status:** Active reference for cluster 6 curation; informs case_arch01_a (PA) and case_arch01_b (DIAG).

## Demographic and Structural Facts

Lalitha is 67 years old, widowed 18 months ago after 42 years of marriage. Her education is a B.A. in Literature from 1978, no formal financial or business training. She has lived her entire adult life as a homemaker, the primary domestic anchor for her late husband (a senior consultant at Apollo Hospital, Chennai, who built the family's wealth) and their only child, a 38-year-old son currently based in Singapore as a software engineer.

She lives in a self-occupied home in Chennai, in a Tamil Brahmin community context that values advisory trust through community references. Her tax structure is individual filer; her late husband's hospital pension flows to her as family pension at Rs 72,000/month. Her city of residence and formative context are both Chennai (Metro). She speaks Tamil at home, reads English for newspapers and correspondence.

Her wealth profile is post-inheritance: Rs 4.2 Cr total net worth of which Rs 3.4 Cr is liquid (FDs Rs 1.8 Cr, equity MFs Rs 1.6 Cr), plus an Rs 80 L second flat in Adyar that she rents out. The MF portfolio was built by her late husband over 20 years and she has never adjusted it. The self-occupied home is separately held and not part of the corpus under advisory consideration.

## Stated Risk Tolerance and Horizon

Lalitha consistently states "conservative, capital preservation". She has articulated this in every onboarding conversation and every quarterly review. She emphasises that the corpus must support her medical needs without dependence on her son's resources. She frames her horizon as "not too long; my son is settled" and resists any framing that includes growth-tier targets or aggressive allocation.

Mandate-stated risk tolerance: Conservative bucket. Mandate-stated horizon: 5-7 years operational; perpetual stewardship beyond that.

## Revealed Behavioural Patterns

Lalitha's revealed pattern is more conservative than her stated profile, which is already conservative. When the 2022 drawdown hit (she had inherited the MF portfolio at the start of that year), she requested partial withdrawal of Rs 30 L from the equity MF position despite her advisor's counsel that the 6-month-window dip would recover. Her advisor at the time honoured the request. The withdrawal locked in a real loss of approximately Rs 4 L.

She accepts FD reinvestment without question, even when better-yielding alternatives are presented. She trusts her advisor (Priya Nair) but defers most active decisions to Priya's recommendation rather than initiating. Her engagement pattern is reactive: she responds to advisor-initiated reviews rather than driving them.

She has never proposed a new product type. She has never asked about PMS, AIF, or any sophistication-tier instruments. When her advisor introduces concepts, she absorbs the explanation politely but rarely follows up with questions.

She reads quarterly reports cover-to-cover but doesn't query the numbers. Her son occasionally asks her to forward statements; he doesn't otherwise engage operationally.

## Family Dynamics

Her son in Singapore is supportive but operationally distant. He visits twice a year for 10-14 days each. He does not assist with portfolio decisions; his stance is "Mom, do whatever you and Priya think is best; I'm not in India enough to be useful". He does worry about her medical preparedness; that worry surfaces in phone conversations and prompted Lalitha to ask Priya for the diagnostic case (case_arch01_b).

Her daughter-in-law is an architect in Bengaluru, separate household. Cordial relationship, no financial entanglement.

Her late husband's brother (her brother-in-law) lives in Chennai but they have minimal financial interaction; community customs preserve appropriate boundaries.

She has household help (cook, driver) who are part of her monthly outflows. She does not have a domestic financial advisor outside Priya.

## Key Tension Points

**Tension 1: Liquidity vs medical contingency.** Lalitha's monthly outflows already run marginally negative before investment income. As medical needs accelerate (potential), the corpus's liquidity profile becomes load-bearing. The advisor's responsibility is to maintain medical-tier optionality without disrupting Lalitha's stated comfort.

**Tension 2: Stated vs revealed risk tolerance.** She states conservative; she behaves more conservative than stated. The advisor must avoid pushing growth-tier framings that would create discomfort, while still honouring fiduciary responsibility to consider growth-equivalent options where appropriate.

**Tension 3: Inheritance corpus emotional context.** The MF portfolio was built by her late husband. Disrupting it (even for clear analytical reasons) may carry emotional weight Lalitha doesn't articulate but that influences her acceptance.

**Tension 4: Son's distance vs her stated independence.** She projects independence ("I don't want to depend on my son's resources") but her son's worry is the trigger for the diagnostic case. The corpus's adequacy must satisfy her stated independence framing while also being truly adequate.
