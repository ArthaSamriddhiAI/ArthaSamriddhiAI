# Character Bible: Shailesh Bhatt (Archetype 08)

**Status:** Active reference for cluster 6 curation; informs case_arch08_a (PA) and case_arch08_b (SCEN failed).

## Demographic and Structural Facts

Shailesh Bhatt (52) is a second-generation textile exporter based in Ahmedabad. He runs the family business (started by his father in 1978), now generating Rs 95 Cr in valuation; Shailesh's share is approximately Rs 48 Cr. The business has both export operations (B2B; cotton fabrics to European and US buyers) and a partial own-brand domestic line (newer initiative, last 8 years).

He is married to Bhavna (49), an English-language teacher at a private school. They have two daughters: Aanchal (24, an MBA finance student at IIM Bangalore, currently in her second year; clear academic and career trajectory) and Ritika (19, undergraduate at NID Ahmedabad, fashion designer aspirations). The daughters are emerging as relevant financial counterparties, particularly Aanchal whose finance training has begun to influence household discussions.

Geographic context: Ahmedabad (Tier 1). Self-occupied home Rs 9 Cr ancestral; second property near Gandhinagar Rs 4 Cr. Cultural context: Gujarati-Jain; second-generation business family with traditional financial conservatism mixed with peer-network pressure (Ahmedabad business community has high PMS/AIF saturation).

Tax structure: individual filer + partnership-firm structures for the textile business. Personal liquid wealth Rs 22 Cr (separate from business equity).

Personal liquid wealth composition: 4 PMS schemes Rs 2 Cr each (Rs 8 Cr total currently Rs 8.7 Cr after market growth); one Cat III long-short fund 2018 vintage Rs 3 Cr current Rs 3.85 Cr; direct equity portfolio Rs 7 Cr; MFs + FDs Rs 4 Cr.

## Stated Risk Tolerance and Horizon

Shailesh stated as Aggressive (verbally); his actual mandate is closer to Moderate-Aggressive given his family obligations and business risk concentration. He frames himself as "I've built a business; I understand risk". His revealed pattern is more conservative.

Mandate-stated risk tolerance: Moderate-Aggressive bucket. Mandate-stated horizon: 12-15 years until handover/retirement; perpetual stewardship beyond.

## Revealed Behavioural Patterns

Shailesh's pattern is the classic Ahmedabad business-family accumulation: relationships drive product additions. The 4 PMS schemes were added progressively over 6 years through different RM relationships. At each addition, the rationale was peer-introduction or RM-pitched track record; not structured analytical evaluation.

He has revealed pattern of trust-by-relationship: each PMS came through a different banker or RM whom he trusted. He has not benchmarked their performance against each other or against alternatives. He receives quarterly statements and reviews them perfunctorily.

The Cat III long-short fund (2018 vintage) was acquired similarly through an RM relationship; never benchmarked against index. CAGR to date 8.2 percent; he believes it's "fine" without comparing.

His direct equity portfolio is large-cap defensive (ITC, HDFC Bank, Reliance) held 7+ years; he doesn't trade.

His MFs are passive-held; no rebalancing in 4 years.

He has demonstrated: stable behavioural floor (no panic during 2022 drawdown); high relationship-trust threshold; low analytical-evaluation engagement; willingness to accumulate complexity without consolidation.

The recent shift: his daughter Aanchal returned home for winter break in December 2025 and reviewed the family corpus. She produced a structured analysis showing the 4 PMS schemes were not differentiated, two were below benchmark on 3-year basis, fees were aggregating to material levels, and the Cat III long-short was not earning its complexity premium.

This catalyst reframed Shailesh's engagement. He came to advisor Priya Nair in March 2026 with the cleanup proposed action.

## Family Dynamics

Bhavna is supportive of Shailesh's decisions; she does not initiate financial conversations. She has expressed implicit concern about the corpus's complexity ("Shailesh, you have so many funds; do you really need them all?") but has not pushed for change.

Aanchal is the catalyst. Her MBA finance education plus her quarterly visits home have introduced structured financial vocabulary to the household. She is currently NOT a co-decision-maker but is an observer-with-influence. Shailesh now consults Aanchal on advisor-recommended decisions; Aanchal pushes back substantively.

Ritika is too engaged with her own design career to participate in financial discussions.

Shailesh's brother (45) operates the business operations side; their financial entanglement is through the partnership, not the personal corpus.

Shailesh's father (passed in 2014) built the business; the corpus inheritance from the father is in the textile business itself, not the personal corpus. The personal corpus was built over 6 years from Shailesh's stake-sale proceeds (2018) plus ongoing dividends.

Bhavna's parents (Bhavnagar, both 75) are mid-class retirees; the Bhatts contribute approximately Rs 3 L/year aggregate. Not a structural drain.

## Key Tension Points

**Tension 2: Aanchal's catalytic role vs her formal authority.** Aanchal is not a co-decision-maker; her influence is through Shailesh; the advisor must engage with Shailesh as the principal but acknowledge Aanchal's analytical input.

**Tension 3: Stated vs revealed risk tolerance.** Stated Aggressive; revealed Moderate. The cleanup represents alignment with revealed pattern (consolidation simplifies; Moderate-fitting).

Aanchal's analysis flagged this; Shailesh has not yet addressed business-personal correlation explicitly.

**Tension 5: Peer network pressure to add complexity vs daughter-driven simplification.** Tension is active; the cleanup is a step toward simplification.
