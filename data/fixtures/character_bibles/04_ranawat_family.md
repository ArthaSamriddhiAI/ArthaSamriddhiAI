# Character Bible: The Ranawat Family, Udaipur (Archetype 04)

**Status:** Active reference for cluster 6 curation; informs case_arch04_a (PA) and case_arch04_b (BRF).

## Demographic and Structural Facts

The Ranawat Family is a third-generation hospitality family with operations centred in Udaipur, Jaipur, and Pushkar. The family's hospitality business runs four operating hotels (3 luxury heritage properties, 1 boutique) plus a hospitality services arm. Business valuation: Rs 340 Cr.

The family unit comprises three branches:
- Eldest son Vijay Ranawat (62) and his wife Rekha (58); two children (Aditya 36, an MBA, currently overseeing Pushkar property; Aanchal 33, a former PR professional, now part-time involved)
- Second son Vikrant Ranawat (58) and his wife Surekha (54); two children (Vivaan 30, the digital marketing lead for the hospitality group; Vihaan 28, in fashion industry, not in family business)
- Daughter Vandana Ranawat (52), unmarried, partial governance role, Director of CSR

Family head: the late Maharaj Sahib (passed in 2018); his grandson Aditya is the de-facto operational heir for hospitality.

A formal Family Trust structure was established 10 years ago (2016) holding Rs 90 Cr distributed across the three branches' children. The trust is professionally administered by Khaitan & Co.

Family investment corpus (separate from hospitality business and the family trust): Rs 74 Cr at family office level. This is the corpus under advisory engagement by Samriddhi.

Geographic context: Udaipur (Tier 2/3) for the family seat; the family home is a heritage haveli (Rs 35 Cr). Cultural context: Rajput (Mewari); deeply traditional with formal hierarchies.

Tax structure: family trust + family office; multiple partnership-firm structures; multi-entity governance. Each branch has its own beneficial-owner reporting.

## Stated Risk Tolerance and Horizon

The family's investment mandate (formalised 2018) states: Balanced bucket with private market alternatives ceiling at 12 percent; equity ceiling 35 percent; debt and structured credit baseline 40-50 percent; liquidity floor 15 percent. Stated horizon: 25-50 years (multi-generational).

The family office governance committee meets quarterly. The committee comprises Vijay (chair), Vikrant, Vandana, the trust's Khaitan & Co. representative, and Samriddhi's CIO Anjali Mehta.

Stated risk tolerance: institutional, multi-vintage, governance-discipline-first. Decisions must support both wealth preservation across generations and operational liquidity for hospitality reinvestment.

## Revealed Behavioural Patterns

The Ranawats have institutional-grade discipline. Quarterly committee meetings produce structured minutes; trustee approval is documented for any allocation above Rs 2 Cr; capital call schedules are integrated into the hospitality cash cycle.

They have multiple Cat II vintages (2018, 2020, 2021, 2023, and now the 2026 vintage from case_arch04_a). The 2018 vintage is in tail (one position remaining at Rs 1 Cr against original Rs 11 Cr; harvested Rs 11 Cr in proceeds over 6 years). The discipline of vintage diversification is locked into governance practice.

Listed equity has been held since 2014-2015 across MFs and direct positions; no panic selling during 2022 drawdown.

They are conservative on commitment-period risk. Vijay personally vetoed a 2024 proposal for a Cat III long-short fund that would have layered commitment-period overlap with two existing Cat II positions; the veto was governance-discipline-first.

The family is fee-conscious but not fee-obsessive. They evaluate fees in absolute terms relative to expected manager value-add, not in isolation.

They are NOT direct-equity-builders. The Rs 110 Cr in Reliance + Rs 100 Cr in TCS + Rs 80 Cr in HDFC Bank were accumulated via committee-approved structured purchases over 2014-2017, not actively traded since.

## Family Dynamics

Vijay is chair of the family office governance committee and de-facto patriarch since the late Maharaj's passing. He is present in 100 percent of quarterly meetings.

Vikrant is operationally engaged in hospitality (general manager hat) plus governance committee member. Lighter on investment matters than Vijay; defers to Vijay's chair role.

Vandana (CSR director) brings social-purpose framing; she has occasionally questioned whether the Ranawat family office should consider impact-themed allocations. The committee has acknowledged but not yet allocated.

The third generation (Aditya, Aanchal, Vivaan, Vihaan) are observed during meetings as future-decision-makers; Aditya has quietly accumulated views and asks pointed questions about Cat II vintage governance. The committee anticipates Aditya joining formal committee membership within 3-5 years.

Khaitan & Co. provides legal-trust governance; ICS Wealth Advisors served as the prior advisor (separated from Samriddhi engagement in 2023).

The hospitality operational team (CFO, CHRO, COO) are not part of investment committee but coordinate liquidity planning with the family office for capex needs.

## Key Tension Points

**Tension 1: Multi-generational governance vs operational urgency.** Hospitality capex sometimes runs ahead of governance approval cycles. The family office must maintain governance discipline without becoming an operational bottleneck.

**Tension 2: Vijay's primacy vs third-generation engagement.** Vijay is 62; succession planning is implicit but not explicit. Aditya's growing engagement signals upcoming transition; the committee culture must accommodate that smoothly.

**Tension 3: Vandana's impact-investing interest vs traditional risk-return discipline.** The committee has heard Vandana but not yet formalised an impact allocation. This will likely surface again.

**Tension 4: Cat II vintage stack management.** With 5 vintages now (2018 wind-down, 2020-2021-2023 active, 2026 new), capital call schedule complexity grows. Reserved liquidity allocation must keep pace.

**Tension 5: Family home (Rs 35 Cr haveli) plus business (Rs 340 Cr) plus family trust (Rs 90 Cr) plus family office corpus (Rs 74 Cr) total exposure to Rajasthan-Udaipur region creates concentration that the family is aware of but treats as identity, not risk.
