## Bible Structure (Six Dimensions)

Each bible covers six dimensions:

1. **Demographic and structural facts:** age, profession, family structure, tax structure, geographic context, AUM tier.
2. **Stated risk tolerance and horizon:** what the client articulates; what the mandate captures.
3. **Revealed behavioural patterns:** how the client has actually behaved; pattern from synthetic prior cases; stated vs revealed divergence.
4. **Family dynamics affecting decisions:** spouse engagement, children's roles, parental dependencies, succession.
5. **Prior decision history (synthesised, 12-24 months):** 8-14 events that establish the archetype's pattern.
6. **Key tension points:** where stated and revealed diverge; where mandate feels tight; where market conditions stress.

Plus a final section of **cross-case consistency anchors:** the specific facts that all cases involving this archetype must respect.

## Curation Use

When curating chunks 6.7-6.10 (per-case full pipeline batched), the curator opens the relevant bible alongside the case story. Every stage output (especially E4 behavioural verdicts and S1 synthesis narratives) must reference the bible's facts consistently.

If a case curation surfaces a behavioural detail that conflicts with the bible, the conflict resolution order per cluster 6 ideation log §8.2:

1. Bible takes precedence over per-case curation.
2. Earlier curation takes precedence over later (within a single curation session).
3. Inconsistencies are resolved by re-curating the inconsistent record against the bible.

## Cross-Case Audit (Chunk 6.11)

The final assembly chunk 6.11 runs a structural cross-case consistency audit: for each repeat archetype, verify that all of that archetype's cases describe the archetype consistently per the bible. Flagged inconsistencies are resolved before fixture assembly closes.

The audit is structural, not deep: it checks behavioural depiction, not analytical correctness. Analytical correctness is the curator's discipline; consistency is the audit's discipline.
